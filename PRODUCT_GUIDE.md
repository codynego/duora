# Duora Product Guide

## Project description

Duora is an experience-led connection platform. It helps people move from a blank conversation to a shared moment, then turns that moment into a reason to keep talking, make a plan, or build a memory.

It is not primarily a game app, a swipe app, or a relationship scorecard. Games and prompts are the entry point; meaningful connection is the outcome.

### Product promise

> Duora helps people create connection—not just pass time.

### Core loop

`Shared experience → conversation → action or memory → next experience`

Not:

`Game → score → leave`

## Product expectations

- Feel playful, flirtatious, warm, and modern—not childish or overly romantic.
- Let people connect with someone they already know **or** discover someone new.
- Make the first interaction low-pressure and consent-led.
- Give every completed experience a meaningful next step.
- Make the product useful for singles, people dating, and couples without turning it into three unrelated products.
- Never rely on endless swiping, fake urgency, or manipulative streaks.

## Launch scope decision

Duora launches for adults aged **18+** with three separate discovery intents:

- **Meet new friends**
- **Light conversation**
- **Dating**

These intents never mix by default. Discovery starts with small, group-first rooms (normally 3–5 people); Duora will not launch with unsolicited one-to-one stranger matching. Private invite experiences remain available for a friend, crush, date, or partner.

Dating requires explicit, controllable age range, location, language, and preference settings. Every discovery path requires block, report, and no-rematch safeguards before public launch.

## Primary user paths

### A. Invite someone already known

For a crush, friend, date, or partner.

1. User chooses **Invite someone**.
2. User selects an experience and a tone: light, playful, thoughtful, or flirty.
3. Duora creates a private room and shareable link.
4. The invitee opens the link in their browser; a guest account is not required to join.
5. Both answer or choose independently.
6. Duora reveals results together.
7. The result offers a useful next action: continue a conversation, save a date idea, try another round, or save a moment.

Private invite rooms never include strangers.

### B. Discover someone new

For a user who has nobody to invite or wants to meet new people.

1. User chooses **Discover people**.
2. User chooses intent: dating, friendship, light conversation, or community.
3. User chooses room tone: light, playful, thoughtful, or flirty.
4. User adds language, availability, and optional interest tags. Dating may also use appropriate age and preference filters.
5. User chooses a Connection Room or joins the next compatible room.
6. After the room, every participant makes private next-step choices.

Discovery is experience-first, not profile-first. Duora should never begin with an empty chat or an endless swipe feed.

## The Connection Room

A Connection Room is a short, structured social session for a small group. It is not an unmoderated group chat, a video call, or a game people win.

### Example rooms

| Room | People | Duration | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| First Spark | 2 | 8 min | One-to-one dating introduction |
| Common Ground | 4 | 12 min | Light conversation and shared interests |
| Weekend Energy | 5 | 10 min | Plans, food, music, places, and interests |
| After Hours | 4 | 15 min | More thoughtful conversation |
| Quiet Company | 3 | 15 min | Gentle, lower-pressure conversation |

### How people are placed

The system should group people in this priority order:

1. Same intent first: friendship users do not enter dating rooms.
2. Same comfort level: light, thoughtful, and flirty spaces remain distinct.
3. Compatible language, availability, relevant filters, and interests.
4. Enough variety to make the conversation interesting; do not make every room a clone of one person.
5. Safety history: never rematch people who blocked, reported, or explicitly passed on each other.

Users can browse a lobby with rooms starting now, starting soon, or scheduled for later. If there are not enough people, communicate a real wait time or offer an asynchronous alternative—never fake availability.

### What happens in a room

Example: a 12-minute Common Ground room.

1. **Arrival (30 seconds):** first/chosen names, non-identifying interest tags, and concise room agreement.
2. **Private response (1 minute):** everyone answers the same light prompt privately.
3. **Shared reveal (2 minutes):** responses appear together; Duora highlights genuine overlap.
4. **Guided conversation (6 minutes):** text conversation with occasional prompts that reduce silence but do not dominate the room.
5. **Closing (2 minutes):** people privately choose their next step.

Every room has a visible, calm timer. The live room closes when time ends.

### Next-step choices

Each choice is private. Users are never told that somebody declined them.

- **Continue chatting:** if both people select this, create a private Connection Thread with shared room context and conversation starters.
- **Open to reconnect:** if both select this, they may be prioritized for another relevant small group room later. This does not create a private chat or share contact details.
- **End here:** no further connection is created.

The preferred progression for cautious users is:

`Meet once → mutual group reconnect → private chat by mutual choice`

## Experiences

### Launch experiences

1. **Predict My Answer** — predict another person’s response before both answers reveal.
2. **Choose Your Move** — make choices in playful social or dating scenarios and discuss the outcomes.
3. **The Chemistry Test** — explore similarities and differences without presenting a fake scientific compatibility score.
4. **Truth or Dare, on your terms** — prompt packs with clear comfort settings and universal skip controls.
5. **Plan Our Date** — compare time, budget, and preferences to create a practical shared plan.
6. **Memory Lane** — couples revisit first impressions, meaningful moments, and future wishes.

### Experience outcomes

Every experience should end with one or more of:

- a thoughtful follow-up question,
- a saved date or activity idea,
- a shared moment,
- a recommendation for a related next experience,
- a private conversation thread when consent permits.

## Different value by relationship stage

### Single person

Primary value: **momentum**.

- Discover people through small timed Connection Rooms.
- Join live, scheduled, or asynchronous rooms based on intent and interests.
- Use solo discovery prompts to form an invitation or Spark Card, but never leave solo work as the only outcome.
- Turn a good group experience into a mutual one-to-one thread.

### Crush / talking stage

Primary value: **a lower-pressure first move**.

- Send a private experience link.
- Use answers and reveals as real conversation material.
- Let people keep the interaction light and safely opt out at any time.

### Couple

Primary value: **meaning and continuity**.

- Open a shared space rather than discover strangers.
- Play date-night, memory, and planning experiences.
- Save answers, date ideas, rituals, and meaningful moments.
- Use recurring invitations as gentle rituals, never gamified streak pressure.

## Authenticated product navigation

Use **Home**, not “Dashboard.”

| Navigation item | Job |
| --- | --- |
| Home | Shows the next best action for the user’s current context |
| Discover | Connection Rooms and experience-led introductions |
| Experiences | Private experiences to send to someone already known |
| Connections | Mutual chats, active Connection Threads, and reconnects |
| Moments | Saved answers, date ideas, memories, and outcomes |
| Profile | Intent, interests, availability, privacy, and safety settings |

On mobile, use: `Home · Discover · Play · Connections · Profile`.

Home adapts to context. A new single user sees a path into discovery; a user with an active thread sees an invitation to continue; a couple sees a shared moment or planning prompt.

## Safety and trust requirements

- A participant can skip any prompt, leave any room, block, report, or unmatch at any time.
- No unsolicited private messaging; private chat requires mutual consent.
- Clearly distinguish group-room visibility from private-thread visibility.
- Use chosen names and avoid exposing sensitive profile details in rooms by default.
- Do not tell users who declined a connection.
- Moderate reports and enforce no-rematch rules.
- Make age, dating preferences, visibility, and comfort settings explicit and controllable.

## Non-goals for the first release

- An endless public swipe feed.
- Public social posting or a follower economy.
- Anonymous unmoderated chat.
- Claims that a score proves compatibility.
- Coins, forced streaks, or reward loops that pressure users to return.
- A feature-heavy couples dashboard before the core shared-experience loop works.

## Success signals

- Users complete an experience rather than abandoning before the reveal.
- Invitees successfully join without installing an app.
- Participants choose a meaningful next step after a room.
- Mutual connections become active conversations or completed follow-up experiences.
- Couples return for a shared activity because it created a real moment, not because of a streak.
- Reports, blocks, and early exits are handled quickly and respectfully.
